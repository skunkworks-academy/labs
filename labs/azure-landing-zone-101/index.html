Azure Landing Zone Essentials - give every workload a governed place to land.

An Azure landing zone is not a one-click template and it is not a single resource group. It is a governed foundation that lets teams build and operate workloads within deliberate identity, policy, networking, logging and cost guardrails.

Module 1

Place each decision at the scope where it can be governed.

An Azure estate is easier to govern when its hierarchy reflects accountable boundaries. Management groups help organise groups of subscriptions under shared governance. Subscriptions provide a practical boundary for billing, access and policy scope. Resource groups organise related resources with a shared lifecycle. Resources provide the actual service capability.

Management group

Groups subscriptions that need common governance and platform direction.

Subscription

Creates an accountable environment, billing and access boundary for a workload or purpose.

Resource group

Organises related resources that share a lifecycle and operational ownership.

Scope matters: a broad control should be applied only where its business and risk requirement is truly common. A workload-specific exception must be documented, reviewed and kept as narrow as possible.

Module 2

Use RBAC and Policy together, not interchangeably.

Role-based access control answers who can take an action at a defined scope. Azure Policy evaluates whether resources meet the organisation’s rules, such as approved locations, required tags or required configuration. A strong landing-zone design uses both alongside operational monitoring, networking and identity controls.

Least-privilege check

  • Use named identities, not shared owner credentials.
  • Choose the smallest required scope and role.
  • Review elevated access and exceptions.
  • Keep policy intent understandable and tested.

Module 3

Treat workload onboarding as a governed product, not a permission request.

A landing zone is valuable when an application team can understand the supported path: where it deploys, what controls apply, what it owns, how it requests an exception, how it monitors the service and who provides platform support. Standardisation reduces insecure one-off configurations and speeds legitimate delivery.

Onboarding checklist

Prepare the production portal to enter the landing zone.

Planning exercise
  • 1
    Define the workload boundary: owner, environment, budget, data classification, criticality and recovery objective.
  • 2
    Apply the standard path: approved region, naming/tagging, identity group, network pattern, log destination and policy assignments.
  • 3
    Validate before release: review role scope, policy compliance, monitoring, backup/restore evidence and cost thresholds.
  • 4
    Run and improve: assign support contacts, document escalation, manage exceptions and periodically re-evaluate controls as requirements change.

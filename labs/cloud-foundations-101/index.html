LAB-CLD-101 · Cloud foundations

Cloud foundations and shared responsibility - choose the service, own the outcome.

Cloud is not a transfer of accountability. A provider runs defined parts of a service; your organisation still owns its data, identities, configuration, workload choices and operating decisions. This lab makes those boundaries visible before you use a real cloud account.

Module 1

Choose a service model by the capability you need to operate.

Service models do not describe good, better and best. They describe how much of the computing stack a customer operates. An IaaS design leaves more operating-system and runtime control with the customer; PaaS removes more platform administration; SaaS provides a finished application whose business configuration remains the customer’s responsibility.

IaaS

Control virtual machines, networks and operating systems.

Use when a workload needs deliberate infrastructure configuration or a legacy dependency. That flexibility also brings patching, hardening, monitoring and lifecycle responsibility.

PaaS

Deploy an application without operating the underlying servers.

Use when the organisation can focus on code, data, configuration and identity rather than maintaining an operating system and runtime platform.

SaaS

Consume a managed application as a service.

Use when a supported business capability already exists. You still govern users, roles, data retention, integrations and secure configuration.

Decision rule: ask what the team must control to meet a documented functional, security or integration requirement. Do not choose IaaS simply because it feels familiar, or SaaS simply because it is quick to procure.

Module 2

Shared responsibility is a map of work, not a transfer of risk.

The exact boundary varies by provider and service. However, customers commonly retain responsibility for data, access decisions, application logic, safe configurations and regulatory duties. A cloud provider may operate a physical facility and an underlying managed service without knowing whether your users should have administrator rights or whether your data-retention setting matches policy.

Module 3

Turn a cloud design into an operating model.

A useful architecture includes more than a diagram. It names the environment boundaries, data sensitivity, identity model, recovery objective, monitoring route, cost owner and escalation path. These are the guardrails that make a service repeatable, auditable and recoverable as it grows.

Design review

Prepare a launch checklist for the fictional learner portal.

No deployment
  • 1
    Classify and isolate: distinguish development, test and production; document the data type and prohibit production data in unapproved environments.
  • 2
    Control access: use named identities, least privilege, MFA where required and periodic review. Do not share administrator credentials.
  • 3
    Observe and recover: define logs, alerts, backup/restore evidence, recovery targets and who is called when the service fails.
  • 4
    Own the cost: establish tags or labels, a budget owner, threshold notifications and a decision path for unexpected spend.

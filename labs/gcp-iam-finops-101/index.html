LAB-GCP-101 · Google Cloud governance

Google Cloud IAM and FinOps guardrails - structure access and spend before scale arrives.

A cloud project is not a personal sandbox by default. It belongs in a resource hierarchy, has accountable ownership, applies least privilege to named principals and must be operated with guardrails for cost, data and lifecycle. This lab turns those ideas into a repeatable review sequence.

Module 1

Place the project in a hierarchy that reflects the responsibility boundary.

Google Cloud resources sit in a hierarchy. An organisation is the root for a managed domain; folders can group projects by an accountable purpose; a project is the working boundary for resources, service enablement, billing association and many access decisions. Inherited policies and access require careful planning because higher-level decisions can affect many projects.

Organisation

Top-level managed boundary. Higher-level IAM and policy can flow down.

Folder

Groups projects that share a governed purpose, such as labs or production workloads.

Project

Contains an accountable workload boundary, resources, labels and service configuration.

Inheritance is powerful: broad permissions or policy exceptions at a higher level can reach more workload teams than intended. Start with the narrowest scope that meets a legitimate governance goal.
The Academy needs a production project for its lab control plane. It should inherit organisation security standards, be grouped with other Academy production workloads and have its own accountable owner and billing visibility. Which placement is most appropriate?

Module 2

Make each access decision explicit: principal, role, scope and time.

IAM answers who is allowed to do what on which resource. A sound access request identifies a named human or workload identity, the approved task, the smallest pre-defined role or permission set, the resource scope, duration and reviewer. Broad owner-style privileges, shared accounts and long-lived keys weaken accountability and make safe recovery harder.

Access review prompts

  • Is the principal named and authorised?
  • Is the role no broader than needed?
  • Is the scope restricted to the workload?
  • Can access expire or be reviewed?

Module 3

Treat cost as a design signal and an operating responsibility.

FinOps connects engineering, finance and business ownership. Labels can help segment eligible resource costs, budgets can alert stakeholders as spend approaches a threshold, and usage reports can reveal a resource or project that needs attention. Budget data can lag and budgets are not, by themselves, an absolute technical guarantee that spending stops; an organisation needs an agreed response and service-specific controls.
